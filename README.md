# Silhouette

A top-down pixel-art survival game that runs in the browser. You are a shadow with twin daggers in a ruined castle. Climb through the castle area by area, 50 waves in all. Get past your own betraying clone, then face the king who never died and take his throne.

The run starts outside the walls on a rainy, moonlit night. The castle is split into areas, each 10 waves long: nine rooms, then the area's boss on the tenth wave.
- **The lower castle** (waves 1–10) always comes first. Wave 1 is the gatehouse, wave 2 the castle yard and wave 3 the graveyard; then the dungeon cells, the wine cellar, the barracks, the kitchen, the armory and the stables in a shuffled order. Its boss is **the Iron Warden**.
- Next come three areas, the learned halls, the flooded undercroft and the spires, in a shuffled order. Waves 11–20, 21–30 and 31–40 are one each, and they change places from run to run.
- **The learned halls**: the library, the scriptorium, the long gallery, the alchemy lab, the chapel, the crypt, the clock tower, the observatory and the archive stacks. Its boss is **the Ember Archmage**.
- **The flooded undercroft**: nine rooms of its own, the pump room, the cistern, the drowned chapel, the sluice gates, the flooded ossuary, the forgotten cells, the old aqueduct, the sunken vault and the well shaft. Its boss is **the Cistern Leviathan**, waiting in the sluice pool.
- **The spires**: the castle's storm-lashed rooftops, nine rooms in the open: the rooftops, the rampart walk, the rookery, the lightning tower, the broken bridge, the weathervane roof, the gargoyle gallery, the chimney stacks and the collapsed roof. Gargoyles wait on its edges. Its boss is **the Storm Rook**, on the rook's roost.
- **The royal halls** (waves 41–50) always come last: the banquet hall, the treasury, the ballroom, the rain-soaked battlements, the hall of mirrors, the hall of knights and the royal baths, plus the nine rooms of **the King's forge**, all drawn from one shared pool so each climb mixes them differently. Wave 45 is always the guard hall, where your clone turns on you as **the Betrayer**, and wave 49 the royal antechamber. Wave 50 is the throne, and **King Aurel the Undimmed**.
- **The bosses speak:** the Iron Warden has nothing left inside to speak with ("It does not speak. It only swings."), the Ember Archmage greets you ("More dark, come to the light."), and a Drowned rises in the sluice pool to whisper what lives there.
- **Don't hug a boss:** every boss hits hard, and if you stay within arm's reach of one for a second it slams the floor around itself (a red ring closes in and the ! goes yellow, then red): parry it on red or roll out. It never does this while it is stuck, stunned or knocked down, so those openings are still yours.
- Every area has nine rooms of its own, so no room comes round twice in an area.
- Beating an area boss deals three Legendary blessings to choose from. Bosses with a blessing of their own put it among the three, once per run.
- **The Iron Warden's blessing, Iron Charge:** your roll becomes a shoulder charge. You still can't be hurt while rolling, but anyone you roll into takes a hit, is knocked back hard and is stunned for a moment. A knight you charge from the front loses its shield, and a soldier driven into furniture or a wall is slammed for extra damage and stays stunned longer. Bosses only take the hit.
- **The Ember Archmage's blessing, Ember Ray:** your Void ray burns orange at its core. Every soldier it touches catches fire and burns for 3 seconds (6 damage a second), and it drips flames onto the floor that last a few seconds and set alight any soldier who walks through them. The flames never burn you.
- **The Storm Rook's blessing, Gale Wings:** your Shadow Blast becomes a wing gust. A pair of shadow wings sweeps out from you and the blast reaches farther, throwing soldiers about two and a half times as far. One flung into a wall or furniture is slammed for extra damage and stays down for over a second; one flung over an open edge falls (or sinks, in the water rooms); and a gargoyle on the wing is knocked out of the air.
- **The Betrayer's blessing, Usurper's Crown:** beating your clone in the guard hall deals three Legendary blessings too, with this one among them. Firing your ultimate makes every soldier in the room kneel for 2 seconds under a gold crown (hits don't get them up any sooner, and mages lose the spell they were casting), and you wear a crown while it burns. Every kill while the ultimate is active adds a second to it, up to 10 extra seconds. Bosses don't kneel.
- **Checkpoints:** there are only two, at wave 20 and wave 40 (each an area boss fight). If you lose all three lives after reaching one, you can carry on from it with three fresh lives and the blessings you had when you got there, or start over from wave 1.
- Enemies keep getting tougher, and the rooms more crowded, all the way up.
- Most rooms have two or three furniture layouts, and one is picked at random each time you enter.
- The order is kept for the whole run, through deaths and reloads.

Outside, rain ripples the puddles and splashes under every footstep, and the outdoor areas have hazards of their own:
- The gatehouse sits behind a moat with a single drawbridge. Walking up to the edge just stops you. A hit that knocks you over it, or a roll that ends above the water, drops you in: you climb back out onto the bank, 12 health lighter. Soldiers on the far side have to come round by the bridge, and any soldier you knock into the moat sinks for good.
- Hay carts come barrelling through the side passages of the castle yard. A rumble and a flickering gold lane warn you first. Step out of the lane or roll through the cart. Soldiers caught in the lane get flattened too.
- Patches of mud in the yard, the graveyard and the gatehouse slow anyone wading through, you and the soldiers alike.

In the stables, the horses are the hazard:
- Horses stand in stalls along the top and bottom walls, rumps to a wide aisle. The patch of floor behind each one glows faintly gold.
- Step into a patch, or let a soldier wander into one, and the horse whinnies, stamps and lays its ears back while the patch flares. Then it kicks: 14 damage to you, and 30 to a soldier. Rolling dodges it.
- Now and then a spooked horse bolts down the aisle from a side passage. A whinny, a rumble and a flickering gold lane warn you first, like the yard's hay carts. Step out of the aisle or roll through it. Soldiers in its way get trampled.

Three rooms of the learned halls have hazards of their own:
- **The clock tower:** a great pendulum swings left and right across the room. Its bob passes low over a dashed gold lane on the floor, flinging you aside for 16 damage and soldiers for 34. Every 20 seconds the bell tolls and for a while it swings wider and faster.
- **The observatory:** a band of pale moonlight sweeps slowly from wall to wall and back. Standing in it exposes you, just like a Lamplighter's lantern.
- **The archive stacks:** three rows of tall shelves on rails, each with one gap to slip through. Every few seconds a row grinds into motion: the gap about to close glows gold, then a shelf slides over it. Anyone still inside is crushed: 18 damage to you, 40 to a soldier.

Three rooms of the royal halls have hazards of their own:
- **The hall of mirrors:** every wall is a mirror. A beam that reaches a side wall or the top wall bounces off it, up to twice, and reaches further than usual. That goes for the mages' fire beams and for your own Void ray, so you can bank a shot round a pillar.
- **The hall of knights:** empty suits of royal armour line the aisle, each over a gold tile. Step onto a tile, or let a soldier step on one, and the suit's eyes glow and the tile flares; then its halberd chops down onto the tile: 18 damage to you, 40 to a soldier. Rolling dodges it.
- **The royal baths:** a steaming pool fills the middle. Grates in the floor hiss (a dashed ring shows the danger) and then blast a column of scalding steam: 12 damage to you, 30 to a soldier. The tiles are wet, so your roll slides about a third further.

Deep in the royal halls lies **the King's forge**. Its rooms share the royal halls' pool, so a climb might pass through a few of them or none. Molten iron runs through some of them. Get knocked into it and you are burned (12 damage), set alight and climb back out; a soldier knocked in is gone. Each forge room has its own hazard as well:
- **The foundry floor:** two molten channels with crossings. Every few seconds the stretch of channel nearest you glows white-hot, then spills over the floor beside it for four seconds. Soldiers standing there are caught in the spill.
- **The crucible gallery:** crucibles ride rails overhead. A red ring marks the floor under a rail near you, the crucible slides over and tips, and a pool of molten iron burns there for a few seconds: 14 damage to you, 30 to a soldier, and anyone walking through it catches fire.
- **The quench room:** troughs of oily water slow you down, and vents at their ends blast scalding steam.
- **The coal store:** a dark, smoky room of coal heaps. Sparks leap from the braziers onto the loose coal (a dashed ring shows where they'll land), and the fire creeps across the floor before it burns down to ash. Stand in it and you catch fire.
- **The slag crust:** plates of black crust over a pool of slag. A plate takes weight for a moment, then its seams glow, then it cracks and drops whoever is on it into the slag. Soldiers in plate armour break through faster. The crust re-forms after a while.
- **The great furnace:** the furnace door opens and it breathes a cone of fire that sweeps across the whole room: 14 damage to you, 30 to a soldier. The pillars are the only shelter, and a roll carries you through.
- **The casting floor:** gutters run from the spout to the moulds. The gutter about to fill lights up first, then molten iron races along it: 12 damage to you, 28 to a soldier caught in its way.
- **The cooling racks:** racks of fresh blades, still glowing. Touch one and it burns you. Two blows topple a rack onto whoever stands beyond it: 45 damage to a soldier, who catches fire.
- **The drive shaft:** a great crank arm sweeps round the whole room from the hub in the middle. Whoever it meets is bowled over (14 damage to you, 25 to a soldier); roll and it passes over you.

Down in the flooded undercroft, the water is the hazard:
- Most of each room is knee-deep water that slows you and the soldiers. Stone walkways, landings and the bases of pillars stay dry, so fight from them when you can.
- **The Drowned** live in the water. Teal rings on the surface show where one is about to rise, and it can't be hurt until it is fully up. They come in three looks: bloated, weed-wrapped and brimming-helm, with water spilling from the visor. They wade at full speed.
- A Drowned that gets its hands on you holds you in chains of water and drains your health. Mash the movement keys to break free (you can't roll while held), and it staggers when you do. Parry its reach and the grab never lands.
- The old aqueduct is crossed by deep channels with narrow footbridges. Fall in and you climb out hurt, and soldiers shoved in sink for good.
- At the sluice gates a current surges across the room on and off, sweeping anyone standing in it sideways.

Up on the spires, the drop is the hazard:
- Every room has open edges: a black drop with the lit courtyard far below. Walking up to an edge just stops you. A hit that knocks you over it, or a roll that ends above it, and you catch the edge and haul yourself back up, 12 health lighter. A soldier you knock over it falls for good.
- **Wind:** every few seconds a gust gathers (thin streaks of rain race across the roof) and then blows hard for a moment, shoving everyone one way. Soldiers are blown about further than you, men in plate half as far, and a soldier blown into an edge goes over it. You can't be blown off, but a gust that pins you against an edge knocks you back from it for 6 damage. Arrows and spells bend with the wind.
- **The rooftops:** now and then a loose slate breaks free at the top of the roof and skids down one of two lanes: 10 damage to you, and a soldier it hits is tripped for 30.
- **The rampart walk:** a long wall walk with the drop on both sides and landings only in front of the gates. The wind blows across it, up or down.
- **The rookery:** run or roll past a crows' nest and the crows burst up cawing. For a couple of seconds you are exposed, as in a Lamplighter's light. Walk slowly past them.
- **The lightning tower:** the storm gathers on three copper rods. Flickering rings mark where it will strike, near the rods and wherever you are standing, and a moment later the bolt lands: 16 damage to you, 35 to a soldier, and twice that to men in plate.
- **The broken bridge:** two roofs joined by a plank bridge. A plank gives way under a man in plate, dropping him and anyone beside him, and swings back into place 7 seconds later.
- **The weathervane roof:** the gusts can come from any side, and the great weathervane in the middle swings round to point the way the next one will blow.
- **The gargoyle gallery:** stone gargoyles line the walk. Three blows crumble one, and the ones that wink were hiding a heal orb.
- **The chimney stacks:** each stack puffs a cloud of soot that drifts and thins out. While you stand in it the soldiers lose sight of you, unless one is right beside you.
- **The collapsed roof:** holes where the roof has fallen in. Every so often the edge of one cracks and flashes, then gives way a little wider, taking down anyone on it.

Every room is lit by its own flames: torches, braziers, candelabras, molten iron, and your own violet glow. Everyone and every piece of furniture throws a soft shadow away from each nearby flame, swinging round as you move past a torch, and pillars, statues and shelves block the light, leaving dark wedges behind them. Out under the sky the moon throws faint shadows of its own.

Each area has its own song: a driving one in the lower castle, a stately march through the learned halls, slow dripping dread in the flooded undercroft, fast wind-blown arps on the spires, and a grand fanfare over forge-hammer drums in the royal halls. In Rush, each room plays its own area's song.

Each room has its own layout and furniture to fight around. Archers can't shoot through shelves, statues or pillars, but their arrows fly over tables, pews and tombs. The exit door is sealed until every enemy in the room is dead. Then it opens and you walk through to the next room. The door out of the room before the guard hall leads to the Betrayer, and the door out of the royal antechamber, after wave 49, leads to the throne room.

After each of the first 48 waves you choose one of three blessing cards. Each card is Common, Rare or Legendary, and rarer cards give bigger numbers. Blessings stack and last the whole run, even through deaths:
- Vitality: more max health
- Keen Edge: more slash damage
- Wellspring: extra energy at the start of each room
- Siphon: stronger heal orbs
- Swiftness: faster movement
- Tumbler: shorter roll cooldown
- Riposte: more energy per parry
- Steady Hand: a longer parry window
- Mending Guard: heal on every parry
- Cruelty: a chance to deal double damage
- Bloodthirst: heal on every kill
- Hunger: the ultimate charges faster
- Focus: cheaper Shadow Blast
- Kinship: the Shadow Clone lasts longer (no longer offered once your clone is gone, after the guard hall)

The pause menu lists your blessings.

The castle fights back in different ways:
- Soldiers attack fast: short wind-ups, quick recoveries, and little rest between swings.
- Swordsmen, spearmen and knights start winding up the moment you step into reach. The glint over their head turns yellow, then red. While it is yellow they turn to follow you. On red they lock in and step into a wide cut, so stepping aside rarely works: roll through it or parry on the red.
- Knights block from the front. Parry one and its shield flies off for good.
- Sappers keep their distance and throw snares onto the floor. A snare is hard to spot (watch for the glint). Step on one and it holds your feet for a moment. Roll across it, or cut it with a slash, a Rending slash or a Blast.
- Mages drop burning circles, or charge a beam that tracks you and then locks on. Step aside once it locks, or roll through it. Tall furniture blocks beams.

From wave 4 the lower castle's kennels are opened:
- **The Kennel-master** wears a horned helm and a war horn, and brings a pack of **hollow hounds**: dogs made of empty armour with ember light glowing between the plates. A pack is two hounds at first and three from wave 7, when some rooms bring two masters. The first time one appears, a banner names it.
- The hounds never stand still. They circle you at a distance, waiting for the horn.
- When the master blows its horn, one hound crouches. A gold lane shows where it will lunge, following you at first and then locking. It springs fast along the lane. Step out of the lane, roll through it, or parry on the red.
- A parried hound is knocked head over heels and lies on its back for a moment, open to anything you have. Hits don't get it up any sooner.
- The master keeps its distance, backing off and stepping aside, but cuts at you with its blade if you get close.
- Kill the master and the whole pack scatters for the gates.

The learned halls fight from range: about half their swordsmen are swapped for archers and mages, and every room brings **Lamplighters** (one at first, two from the fifth room of the area):
- Hollow acolytes in purple robes with a gold cord, carrying a lantern on a long pole. A dotted gold ring on the floor shows how far its light reaches. The first time they appear, a banner names them.
- While you stand in the light you are **exposed**: every hit on you does 30% more damage and your energy drains away. A Lamplighter follows you a few steps off to keep you lit.
- A heavy blow knocks the lantern off its pole: the last cut of your combo, a Rending slash, a parry, a Shadow Blast or an Iron Charge. It smashes and spills burning oil, and the acolyte keeps fighting in the dark, jabbing with the bare pole. Its jab can be parried like a soldier's swing.

From the second room of the royal halls, the King's guard march with **Standard-bearers** (one at first, two from the sixth room of the area):
- Hollow guards in a red tabard with a gold stripe and a red plume, carrying the royal banner on a tall pole. A dotted gold ring on the floor shows the banner's reach. The first time they appear, a banner names them.
- Every soldier inside the ring is **rallied**: it winds up its swings about 30% faster, swings more often and hits 30% harder. Gold motes rise off rallied soldiers.
- The bearer hangs back a few paces behind whichever soldier is fighting closest to you, so the ring covers the front line, and backs away if you close in. It jabs with the pole if you corner it, and the jab can be parried.
- Kill the bearer, or knock the banner off its pole with a heavy blow (the last cut of your combo, a Rending slash, a parry, a Shadow Blast or an Iron Charge), and the ring goes out. With the banner down, the bearer just comes at you with the bare pole.

From the second room of the spires, **Gargoyles** wait on the roof edges (one at first, two from the sixth room of the area):
- Crouched stone gargoyles with horns, a fanged grin and folded wings, sitting on a plinth by the drop. The first time they appear, a banner names them.
- While perched they are solid stone: blows just clang off. Come near (or hit one, or wait a few seconds) and its eyes flicker red, it cracks awake and takes wing.
- On the wing it flies over the open sky, rides the gusts instead of being blown off, and circles at a distance. Then it hangs in the air drawing back (yellow !) and swoops in a straight dive (red !).
- If the swoop lands it snatches you and drags you toward the nearest edge. Mash a direction to tear free, or knock it loose with a heavy blow. If it reaches the edge it lets go: you catch the lip and haul yourself back up, 20 health lighter.
- Roll through the swoop, or parry it: a parry clips its wing and drops it onto the slates for good, where it fights on foot, slowly, like a soldier. Parry it while it is over the drop and it falls.
- Stay on the roof's own stone: a gargoyle that snatches you far from any edge just has to let go.

From the third room of the royal halls, the King's **Smiths** come up from the forge to fight (one at first, a Hammerer and a Riveter together from the seventh room of the area). They swore the oath with everyone else: soot-black arms, a leather apron and a hood, ember eyes, and no shadow at their feet. The first time they appear, a banner names them.
- **The Hammerer:** broad and slow. He plods up to you and lifts his sledgehammer overhead while a dashed ring shows how far the blow will reach. The head glows white-hot, then he slams it down and a ring of sparks rolls out across the floor (16 damage). Roll through the ring as it reaches you, or be well outside it.
- **The Riveter:** keeps his distance, with a brazier on a pole at his side. He lobs three red-hot rivets at once; dashed rings show where they'll land. A rivet that lands on you hurts, and each one lies hot on the floor for a few seconds, burning whoever steps on it. That includes soldiers, who catch fire, so lead them across.

Only three melee fighters press in on you at once; the rest circle at a distance and wait for an opening.

From wave 5 on, some soldiers are elites: one per room at first, then two, then three. An elite has a coloured outline and glow, a small diamond over its head, and its trait called out when it arrives. Elites are tougher, and each one drops a heal orb and 20 energy when it falls:
- Burning (orange) leaves a trail of fire. Its fire and its hits set you alight for a few seconds. Roll across the flames to stay clear of them.
- Swift (cyan) moves faster and swings sooner.
- Splitting (green) bursts into two small copies when it dies.
- Vampiric (red) heals whenever it hurts you or your clone.

About one room in three has a twist, announced right after the wave banner. Twists never come twice in a row, and never in the first three waves or the last one:
- Lights out (indoor rooms only): the torches and candles gutter and die as the fight starts. You see by your own glow and the soldiers by their ember eyes. The lights come back once the room is cleared.
- A captain leads them: a big captain with a gold outline and a pennant on its back leads the room. Kill it and every soldier left breaks and runs for the gates. The rest of the wave never comes, and the captain drops two heal orbs and 50 energy.

## The Cistern Leviathan

The undercroft's boss is a huge deep-water eel living in the sluice pool, dark green, with rusted gate plates and broken chains still bolted to its body, and red ember eyes. You fight it from the stone walkways:
- It swims under the water, where only its dark shape and two red eyes show, then surfaces near you. It can't be hurt while it is under or still rising.
- **The lunge:** it draws its head back and a gold lane flashes across the floor. The lane follows you at first, then locks. Roll out of it. If the lunge ends on stone, its head is stuck there for a couple of seconds, and that is your chance to hit it. Parry the lunge on the red and it is stunned for longer.
- Its coils break the surface behind it. Slash them for a little damage when you can't reach the head.
- **Collapsing walkways:** now and then it thrashes, and the stretch of walkway nearest you starts to crack (orange lines that flicker faster and faster). A moment later it falls into the pool, hurting anyone standing on it. The landings by the doors and the crossing in the middle always hold.
- **Below half health** it wakes fully: the Drowned rise around you, it lunges twice in a row, and the water climbs over the edges of the walkways, so more of the floor slows you.
- **Its blessing, Deep Bite:** when it falls, one of the three cards is its own boss blessing. Your Rending slash lunges much farther, and the first soldier it bites is pinned in chains of water for 1.5 seconds. It is offered once per run.

## The Storm Rook

The spires' boss is a giant storm-black rook on the rook's roost, a wide rooftop with an iron lightning rod in the middle and four chimneys around it. As you walk in, the crows caw words at you ("...the King's eyes...").
- **On the rod:** it starts perched high on the lightning rod, out of reach. Your blows miss ("OUT OF REACH"). It caws, the rod's copper crown glows, and bolts come down on rings that mark where they will strike. Keep moving.
- **Gale wings:** it spreads its wings and beats a gale that pushes you toward the open edges. Get behind a chimney or the rod and the wind can't touch you. Caught near an edge, you teeter and take damage.
- **The dive:** a ring marks you, then it dives at that spot and lands. While it walks and pecks on the roof, you can hit it. Parry a peck on the red and it is thrown down, stunned, for a big opening. Then it flies back to the rod.
- **Below half health the storm breaks:** it leaves the rod for good and circles the roof. Bolts fall on their own, twice as often. Between gusts it strafes: a gold lane flashes across the roof, then it sweeps down it. Roll out of the lane.
- **Its blessing, Gale Wings:** when it falls, one of the three cards is its own boss blessing: your Shadow Blast becomes a wing gust that hurls soldiers into walls and off the edges. It is offered once per run.

## The Betrayer

On wave 45 you walk into the guard hall and your own clone turns on you: "Forty-five waves. Every blow you dodged, I took. That throne was mine before you had a shape. You will not climb one step higher." It wears your appearance, with red eyes. Beat it and blessings are dealt as after any room, and the door opens on the last four rooms before the throne. The room after it is a checkpoint.

## King Aurel the Undimmed

Wave 50 is the throne room. As you walk up to the throne, the Lamp on it flares, and an old king in white robes steps out of its light: "Thank you. You brought them home. Every shadow you cut down came back to the Lamp. Now you." He is the last fight, and beating him crowns you. He grows stronger with every blessing you carry in.
- **The Lamp's light burns.** He raises the Lamp and the room fills with light. Only the shadows behind the pillars are safe: they show on the floor while the light gathers, so get behind a pillar before the flare.
- He sweeps a beam of white light across the room. Pillars stop it; roll through it or get behind one.
- Get too close and he swings the Lamp like a mace. Parry it on the red, or roll out.
- Empty armour from the climb rises around him: two soldiers, then three in his second phase.
- **Below a third of his health** he sets the Lamp down before the throne. While it burns it takes most of every blow meant for him. Break it and the room goes dark: the armour falls, and he is only an old man rushing at you in the dark (watch the line, then roll or parry).
- He drops no blessing: beating him ends the climb.

## The Twin Captains

In Rush mode two captains who fight as a pair guard every room ending in 5 (rooms 5, 15, 25 and so on):
- The Shield Captain is a huge knight that blocks from the front. Parry its bash to knock the shield away for a few seconds.
- The Blade Captain darts in for quick flurries of cuts, then falls back behind the shield. It also lunges from a distance: watch for the crouch, then sidestep or parry. After a lunge it is open for a long moment.
- Kill one and the other rages: faster, harder and relentless.

No two soldiers are quite alike. Each suit of hollow armor has its own helmet, scrap of cloth, rust, dents, eye glow and build (tall, stocky or hunched). While they wait their turn they fidget: some breathe, some glance around, some tap a foot or flip a dagger. They talk, too: speech bubbles pop up when they spot you, get hit, fall, get parried or cast, and they throw taunts at you mid-fight.

Your twin daggers are black shadow blades wrapped in curling purple tendrils, with threads of shadow drifting off them. Every slash is a stroke of pure shadow ink with a purple rim, and it unravels into wisps as it fades.

Each ability has its own sound:
- Shadow Blast is an implosion: a quick rush of air, then a deep boom.
- The Rending slash is an ink whip.
- The Shadow Clone is a whoosh that splits in two.
- The Void ray starts with a low surge, hums with a deep crackling drone while you hold it, and fizzles out when it ends.
- The ultimate is a heavy drop under a dark, rising chord.

## The Chronicle

The castle remembers what happened to it. Every room hides one torn page: a gatekeeper's ledger, a royal decree, a prisoner's words scratched into a cell wall, the Archmage's working notes.

Each page has to be earned with that room's own mission, and you have to work out what it is. Every room has a riddle, but you won't see it during the fight: open the Chronicle (from the pause menu) to read it. Your HUD only shows a short task name and your progress.

Some missions are things to find or do in the room. The marked things don't glow; they only shimmer faintly once you're right beside them, so search the places the riddle points to. Others are fighting challenges, judged as you go. The Warden, the Archmage and the Leviathan have missions of their own.

Room tasks can still be finished after the room is cleared. When a mission is done, the page falls, glowing on the floor; walk over it to take it. Fail a mission and the page is lost for this run. It waits for the next time that room comes up.

There are 59 pages in all:
- 54 are in the story rooms: 9 down in the undercroft, 9 up on the spires and 9 in the King's forge.
- 1 lies on the throne itself, after the King falls.
- 4 are with the Iron Warden, the Ember Archmage, the Cistern Leviathan and the Storm Rook.

Missions are hard to finish on the first try, so finding every page takes several climbs. Pages are kept for good, through deaths and new runs. Read them in the Chronicle, from the title screen or the pause menu. A page you haven't found yet shows the room it lies in and its clue.

Nobody in the castle knows what you are. The pages only hint.

## Appearance

Choose how your shadow looks from Appearance on the title screen: the classic shade, the hooded wanderer, the broken crown, the masked assassin, the smoke wraith, the horned shade or man face. Man face wears a big round head with a famously smug face, drawn sharp on top of the pixel art so every line of it shows. A preview shows each one walking and slashing. Your choice is saved, and your clone and the Betrayer wear it too.

## Rush mode

Rush mode is an endless run from the title menu. The rooms come in random order and the enemies keep getting tougher. Elites show up from room 3, with one more every 6 rooms (up to four), and room twists can strike from room 4. You have one life and every move from the start, and you choose a blessing after every room. The Twin Captains hold every room ending in 5. Every 10th room is a boss, and the two bosses take turns, coming back stronger each time around. Beating one gives you a choice of three Legendary blessings. Your best room is saved.

- **The Iron Warden** (rooms 10, 30, 50...) is a giant hollowed knight in blackened plate, ember light leaking from every seam, one horn and its greatsword snapped short. It never just swings at you; it uses an ability every few seconds. It lowers its greatsword and charges: parry the impact and its guard breaks, leaving it open to extra damage (or roll aside, and if it hits a wall it reels). It drives the sword into the floor to raise lines of iron pillars toward you; each spot cracks and glows first, so step off the marks. And it leaps and slams the floor: roll just as the shockwave ring reaches you.
- **The Ember Archmage** (rooms 20, 40, 60...) teleports around the room and sweeps a turning beam across it; run ahead of the beam, hide behind a pillar or roll through it. It also rains meteors in waves. It blinks away when you crowd it, but after each spell it is spent for a moment.

Both have three phases and get faster in each. Each has its own theme: hammer-blow drums and anvil clinks for the Warden, rippling ember arpeggios for the Archmage. Both speed up in the last phase.

The Betrayer grows stronger with every blessing you carry in, so a stacked run still gets a real fight. It doesn't slash at you; it uses an ability every couple of seconds. It throws fans of shadow daggers (parry one to send it back into the Betrayer, or roll through). After a red flash at your feet it blinks in behind you and grabs: roll away before the red ring closes or it pins you in place. In its second phase it sends shadow waves rolling across the floor. In its third it splits into shadow copies that lock on and dash through you. Roll through them or step out of the line. Its shadow blast can be rolled too: roll as the red ring closes in.

## Controls

| Input | Action |
| --- | --- |
| W A S D | Move |
| Left click | Slash. Every swing needs its own click (unless School mode is on, see below). Click fast to chain the combo (every 3rd swing is a heavy finisher). A click up to 3 frames early, at the end of a roll, a parry or the previous swing, still counts |
| Right click | Parry when the ! above an enemy turns red (+30 energy, up to +50 for parries in a row without getting hit). A good parry is ready again at once, so you can answer every hit of a combo. After a clean parry your guard holds a quarter of a second longer, so the next attacker in line is parried too, up to the third; then press again. Every parry also makes the soldiers crowding round you flinch back a few steps before they come again. |
| Space / X | Roll: you're untouchable mid-tumble, so arrows and strikes miss. Rolling through an attack that would have hit you gives +25 energy (once per roll) |
| E | Shadow Blast (50 energy) |
| C | Rending slash (25 energy, unlocks on wave 3) |
| R | Shadow Clone (100 energy, unlocks on wave 5, lost for good once it turns on you in the guard hall) |
| V (hold) | Void ray (10 energy, then 20 a second, up to 50; unlocks on wave 8) |
| 1 | Ultimate, once the bar is full (20 kills) |
| Esc / P · M | Pause · Sound and music |

These are the default keys. Every action can be moved, so the whole game can be played on the keyboard alone (see Key bindings below).

**School mode:** a toggle in the bottom-right corner of the title screen. With it on, holding the slash button keeps swinging as fast as clicking would, so you don't have to click for every swing. The setting is remembered.

## Key bindings

Open Key bindings from the title screen or the pause menu (you can change keys mid-run). Every action has two slots, a main and an alternate: move up, down, left and right, slash, parry, roll, Shadow Blast, Rending slash, Shadow Clone, Void ray, the ultimate and pause.
- Pick a slot, then press any key or mouse button (left, middle, right or the side buttons). Esc cancels, and Delete clears the slot.
- A key that is already in use swaps places: the action it came from gets the slot's old key, so nothing is ever bound twice.
- Slash and parry work on keys exactly as they do on the mouse. Each key press is one swing, and holding the key does not repeat.
- Esc always pauses and backs out of menus, and M always turns the sound on and off, so those two can't be rebound.
- Reset to defaults brings back the keys in the table above.
- Your bindings are saved in the browser. The HUD key boxes, the controls lists and the in-game tips all show your own keys.

The ! parry warning always draws on top of everything else. If it would land under a HUD panel or another warning, it slides clear and a thin line points back to the enemy it belongs to.

Slash, parry and roll sit in the top right. The moves that cost energy sit in a bar at the bottom center, with your energy underneath. Shadow Blast is there from the start; each other move unlocks with a banner when you reach its wave:
- Rending slash: lunge forward and cut through every enemy in a line. It staggers them and knights can't block it.
- Shadow Clone: summon a shadow that fights beside you for a while. After your clone turns on you in the guard hall, it never answers again: the slot is crossed out and reads GONE for the rest of the story (Rush mode keeps it).
- Void ray: hold the key to pour a thick beam of shadow from your hands for up to 2 seconds. It starts on the nearest soldier ahead and swings slowly toward the way you walk; you can only shuffle while channelling. It hits everything along it five times a second (10 damage a hit, so up to 100), goes through a knight's shield, cuts arrows out of the air and snaps snares. Tall furniture stops it. It costs 10 energy to start and 20 a second while you hold it, and ends when you let go, run dry or reach 2 seconds.

Every 3 kills a green heal orb drops near you. Walk over it for +15 HP and +25 energy before it fades (8 seconds). Energy past 100 isn't wasted: every 25 extra counts as a kill toward your ultimate. The final boss drops one at 75%, 50% and 25% health.

The ultimate bar fills as you kill. Press 1 to wreathe yourself in shadow flame for 15 seconds or until the wave ends. Your slashes reach much farther, you move faster, and a 10-point shield takes the edge off the first hit. For its first 5 seconds your health climbs back fast enough to go from empty to full, so it is best saved for when you are hurt.

Plays with a mouse and keyboard, or with the keyboard alone after rebinding slash and parry. The whole game is the single file `index.html`.
